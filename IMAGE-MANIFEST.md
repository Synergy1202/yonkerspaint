## Hero candidates, ranked

Scored on the thing that actually matters for a hero: **is there a quiet region large enough to hold a headline without a heavy scrim?**

**1. `dept-paint-color-wall-01.webp`** (DSC05258) — **recommended homepage hero.** The chip wall sweeps diagonally from saturated orange into teal, and the right-hand third falls away into genuine optical blur. That blur is a ready-made text bed: a headline can sit there at full contrast with no scrim at all. It is also the most on-brand frame in the library — a Benjamin Moore dealer's colour wall says "paint store" instantly, with no signage needed. Landscape 3:2 crops cleanly to a wide banner.

**2. `dept-paint-color-wall-03.webp`** (DSC05268) — Strong second. The Benjamin Moore Classics wall recedes hard down its length and the left two-thirds is a smooth cream-to-yellow gradient that reads calm at a distance. Two caveats: it is uniformly *light*, so it only takes dark text; and the right third is busy with sale signage and would need cropping or a gradient. Its advantage over #1 is the legible "Benjamin Moore Classics" header, which does brand work for free.

**3. `storefront-exterior-01.webp`** (DSC05371) — The identity shot, but a **poor text backdrop**. The facade already carries the store's own large lettering plus three window signs, so any overlaid headline fights existing type. Use it where it can be the whole statement — About header, Contact block, the top of the location section — rather than under a headline. One thing to check: the leading word of the sign is clipped at the left frame edge, so the fascia reads "…PAINT & HARDWARE" rather than the full store name.

**4. `dept-paint-color-wall-02.webp`** (DSC05257) — The portrait sibling of #1, and the reason to keep both: it is the natural mobile/tall crop when the wide hero would centre-crop badly.

**5. `service-key-cutting-01.webp`** (DSC05236) — Great storytelling, weak as a backdrop. Detail runs corner to corner (key blanks above, machinery below) with no quiet zone. Best as a full-bleed section image with text beside it, not over it.

**Not hero-worthy despite being good photos:** every aisle and shelf frame in the set. They are uniformly dense from edge to edge — that density is exactly what makes them good *department* images and bad *headline* images.

### Gaps — what still needs shooting or sourcing

1. **Electrical — zero coverage.** Nothing in the 50 masters shows outlets, switches, bulbs, wire spools, breakers, extension cords or a lighting display. `dept-fasteners-pegboard-01.webp` *may* contain electrical connectors but I cannot confirm it, and it is not usable as a department image. This department has a live page and a nav entry with nothing to illustrate it. **Highest-priority reshoot.**

2. **Garden / seasonal — zero coverage.** No hoses, sprinklers, shovels, rakes, planters or lawn products. `dept-cleaning-brooms-01.webp` has long-handled tools but they read as janitorial, not garden.

3. **Snow / ice melt — zero coverage.** No rock salt, ice-melt pallets, snow shovels, roof rakes or spreaders. Predictable from the shoot conditions — the storefront shows a leafy tree and staff in short sleeves, so this was a warm-weather session. **This one needs a winter reshoot, so it should be scheduled now rather than discovered in November.**

4. **Tools — weak, and the gap is specific.** `dept-tools-accessories-01.webp` covers bits, blades and abrasives well, but there is **no frame of a power-tool display** — no Milwaukee or DeWalt wall, no cordless kits, no bare tools. For a store that leads with those brands, the tools department currently has to be illustrated by its accessory aisle. `dept-tools-dewalt-display-01.webp` is a DeWalt *banner* over a shelf of aerosols, so it credits the brand without showing a single tool.

5. **No paint-mixing or colour-matching in action.** The service coverage is lopsided: key cutting has four frames including a genuine in-action shot, while **spectrophotometer colour matching and can tinting — the headline service for a Benjamin Moore dealer — has none.** There is no shaker, no tinting machine, no chip being scanned. This is the most valuable single frame missing from the library.

6. **No delivery, pipe-threading, or contractor/bulk imagery**, all of which the services page currently claims.

7. **Two selected frames show prices.** `service-key-wall-signage-01` shows "Standard Keys $2.00" — confirmed current by the owner on 2026-08-30 and now placed. `dept-paint-aura-shelf-01` shows shelf-edge sale tags that have **not** been confirmed; it remains placed on the paint department page and is worth a look if those tags change.

## Reproducing the web set

`triage/` holds the scripts (gitignored along with `node_modules`):

- `triage/inventory.js` — hashes, dimensions, corruption check → `triage/inventory.json`
- `triage/sheets.js` — numbered contact sheets → `triage/sheets/sheet-01..03.jpg`
- `triage/zoom.js`, `triage/hero.js` — larger review grids for ambiguous groups
- `triage/build.js` — holds the source→name map and writes `images/*.webp` at q80 / 1600 px

`node triage/build.js` regenerates the full web set from `dropbox/` at any time. Other sizes (thumbnails, 2× retina, AVIF) can be produced from the same masters by changing the resize and format calls.
